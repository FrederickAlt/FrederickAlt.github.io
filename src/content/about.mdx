## Frederick Altrock

PhD researcher and software engineer at the University of Münster, focused on **applied AI/ML systems**, **neural-network training dynamics**, and the **probability theory** behind how neural networks learn.

### Who am I?

I like work that connects mathematical ideas with systems that can actually be built, tested, and improved. In my doctoral research, I study convergence and training dynamics of ultra-wide neural networks, especially how perspectives such as **NTK**, **$\mu P$**, rich-regime training, lazy-regime training, and grokking fit together.

Alongside research, I enjoy hands-on engineering projects across the AI and systems stack: PyTorch/CUDA tooling for neural-network visualization, custom hardware, and practical software for maker projects. I am especially drawn to problems where mathematical insight, careful engineering, and low-level constraints meet.

I am also interested in IT security, reverse engineering, and low-level programming. That curiosity often shows up in projects where I take opaque systems apart, understand how they behave, and rebuild or extend them in a cleaner way.

I also enjoy 3D printing. It allows me to prototype ideas quickly, iterate on designs, and build practical parts for my own projects. I wouldn’t want to give that up, so you will find a few posts on this too.
